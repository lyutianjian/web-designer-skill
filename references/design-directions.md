# Design Directions

Use when choosing or changing a visual language, comparing alternatives, or resolving a consequential design tradeoff. Existing design context may already settle these decisions.

## Read the brief

Ground the direction in the audience, primary task, content, viewing conditions, and supplied references. Read existing components for implementation constraints and renders for their visible effect. Explain consequential choices briefly; a scorecard or separate design document is unnecessary unless it helps the task.

| Dimension | Decisions it should inform |
|---|---|
| Composition | Familiar grids support scanning; expressive layouts can establish identity when navigation and reading order remain clear. |
| Motion | State feedback, transitions, and narrative need different amounts of movement. Match motion to its purpose and the user's preferences. |
| Density | Operational tools need comparison and scanning; presentations need legibility at a distance. Preserve useful information when changing its arrangement. |
| Imagery | Determine whether real products, interfaces, photography, or illustration carry the message, or whether type and data can carry it. |
| Brand fidelity | An extension should feel native. A broader redesign can change the visual language while preserving the identity and behavior the brief retains. |

## Resolve tradeoffs

- Expressive composition with dense information benefits from stable navigation and grouping. Concentrate novelty where it strengthens the message.
- Dense interfaces benefit from motion that explains a change or directs attention, rather than animating every element.
- Recognition depends on the relevant assets and characteristic relationships, not just copying a palette. Use current supplied materials when fidelity matters.
- Accessibility and content needs can justify departing from an inspirational reference. Judge the resulting experience, rather than fidelity to an arbitrary score.

## Explore when it reduces uncertainty

A small comparison can clarify an unresolved direction. Vary meaningful properties such as hierarchy, density, image treatment, or interaction; cosmetic recolors rarely answer a design question. Choose the number and fidelity of options to fit the uncertainty.

Describe each option through concrete visual cues and why it suits the brief. A named brand or designer can be a useful reference, but an original direction is equally valid. If the user delegates the choice, choose and proceed; a preview can gather feedback without becoming an approval gate.

Use the [style index](style-recipes/INDEX.md) when inspiration or comparison is useful. A named reference can be opened directly. Catalog values are illustrative starting points, and combining cues is reasonable when the result is coherent.

## Assets and visual studies

Use supplied assets or official sources for identity-critical material. Verify product facts when they affect the design's claims. Keep missing material visibly provisional and continue work that does not depend on it; clarify only a gap that prevents a meaningful result.

Image studies can help resolve composition or art direction for an image-led page. Use them when they reduce uncertainty, and use available image tooling only if needed. A supplied design, a bounded UI repair, or an operational dashboard often provides enough direction already. Extract useful composition and image-treatment decisions from a study while preserving real text, assets, and interaction requirements.
