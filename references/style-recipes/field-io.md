# field-io — FIELD-Inspired Generative Identity

Illustrative cues, not an official brand specification. Supplied references and accessibility needs take precedence.

**Context**: Motion / Experimental. Kinetic identity, agency work, and visual systems whose behavior helps express the brand.
**Reference points**: FIELD and FIELD.SYSTEMS project studies, especially the relevant kinetic identity.

## Starting palette and type

- Dark ground examples: `#0B0B0F` or `#000000`; foreground `#FFFFFF`; secondary text `#A0A4B0`.
- Cyan `#0CE0E5` and violet `#5B2EFF` can form a generative color relationship; adapt hues to the project.
- A variable display face can support changes in weight, width, or another available axis. Check the selected font's actual axes before designing around them.
- A quiet reading face can keep explanatory text clear alongside expressive type.

## Composition cues

- Let an underlying rule connect changing forms, type, color, and timing. The output should feel related across different states.
- Type can resolve from a field, change shape, or reveal a hierarchy in response to meaningful progress.
- Cursor and scroll input can influence a focal system while controls retain predictable behavior.
- Staggered entry, controlled easing, and transitions between open and dense compositions can establish rhythm.
- Lighting and depth may come from canvas, WebGL, CSS, or image treatment according to the intended result.

## Tradeoffs

A motion reference can also inform a static composition through framing, contrast, and a captured state. Keep text readable, avoid unnecessary high-frequency updates, and offer reduced-motion behavior that preserves content. Choose complexity from the task and device constraints rather than assuming a large production budget.

## Optional image study
