# bloomberg-terminal — Bloomberg Terminal-Inspired Density

Illustrative cues, not an official brand specification. Supplied references and accessibility needs take precedence.

**Context**: Information Architecture. Monitoring, finance, operations, and interfaces for frequent comparison.
**Reference points**: Actual terminal workspace captures for the relevant task and period.

## Starting palette and type

- Ground `#0A0E1A`; surfaces `#11172A` and `#1A2138`; dividers `#2A3050`.
- Amber `#FFA02F` for emphasis; foreground `#E8ECF4`; muted labels `#5E6680`, adjusted as needed for contrast.
- Example positive/negative colors: `#00B96B` and `#F23645`. Follow domain and locale conventions, and pair color with signs or labels.
- Mono options: IBM Plex Mono, JetBrains Mono, or Berkeley Mono. Tabular numerals and aligned units matter more than using monospace for every label.

## Composition cues

- Spacing example: 2 / 4 / 8 / 12 for compact data groups; provide larger separation between tasks where useful.
- Multi-pane workspaces and thin dividers can support simultaneous monitoring. Choose the pane count from the user's work and viewport.
- Make updates visible through clear state changes or a restrained highlight. Tickers and scrolling regions need readable pacing and usable controls.
- Show keyboard shortcuts when they correspond to implemented actions.
- Distinguish primary values, labels, and supporting context through stable alignment and emphasis.

## Tradeoffs

Density is useful when users need the information. Preserve readable text, discoverable actions, and responsive access rather than filling every pixel. Marketing pages may borrow a focused workspace capture without adopting the entire terminal layout. Clearly labeled sample data is appropriate for a prototype.
