# mid-century-modern — Mid-Century Graphic Modernism

Illustrative cues, not an official brand specification. Supplied references and accessibility needs take precedence.

**Context**: Specialty / Genre. Cultural, film, publishing, and identity work informed by geometric illustration and print craft.
**Reference points**: Paul Rand, Saul Bass, Alvin Lustig, and Reid Miles; select the specific visual relationships relevant to the project.

## Starting palette and type

- Cream `#EBE3D2`; mustard `#D9A441`; teal `#3D6E70`; ink `#1A1A18`.
- Pairing examples: mustard, brick, and cream; teal, cream, and black; sienna, ink, and bone. A limited palette can emphasize shape without imposing a fixed color count.
- Futura, Avenir, or ITC Avant Garde can support geometric display type; a slab serif can change the editorial tone.
- Sabon or Garamond can support reading text where appropriate. Numerals can become graphic elements when they carry actual meaning.

## Composition cues

- Spacing example: 4 / 8 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48, organized around a strong underlying structure.
- Circles, wedges, half-moons, and deliberately irregular silhouettes can carry the main idea.
- Type can interact with a shape or inhabit a color field while remaining readable.
- Paper texture and slightly imperfect edges can evoke print without overwhelming fine detail.
- A recurring motif can connect sections. Simple entrances or scene changes can borrow the rhythm of graphic title sequences.

## Tradeoffs

Photography, gradients, curved controls, and additional colors can fit an adaptation when they serve the content. A feature-dense application may borrow illustration and palette while retaining a practical working layout. Use real assets when an identifiable product must be shown accurately.
