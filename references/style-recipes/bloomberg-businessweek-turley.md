# bloomberg-businessweek-turley — Turley-Era Businessweek-Inspired Editorial

Illustrative cues, not an official brand specification. Supplied references and accessibility needs take precedence.

**Context**: Brutalist / Raw. Opinionated features, cultural commentary, and expressive editorial compositions.
**Reference points**: Richard Turley-era Bloomberg Businessweek covers and related experimental magazine work.

## Starting palette and type

- Accent examples: orange `#FF3D00`, yellow `#FFE800`, and blue `#001AFF`, grounded by white `#FFFFFF` or black `#000000`.
- A limited palette can strengthen the composition; the appropriate number of colors depends on content and semantic needs.
- Display options: Founders Grotesk, Druk, or ABC Whyte at emphatic weights. Plantin or Mercury can provide quieter reading text.
- Extreme scale and tight tracking can create editorial tension while the complete message stays readable.

## Composition cues

- Let large type cross an apparent grid or meet the edge when the crop is deliberate and the text remains understandable.
- Flat color fields and photographic cutouts can connect headline and image into one composition.
- Unexpected caption placement can add character when readers can still associate it with the correct image.
- Data and quotations can receive strong editorial emphasis without altering their meaning.
- Abrupt transitions may suit the tone, but static layouts and restrained motion can carry the same visual energy.

## Tradeoffs

Expressive disorder needs a clear reading hierarchy. Preserve accessible text, purposeful controls, and responsive behavior even when the composition challenges convention. Utility-heavy screens may borrow a headline treatment while keeping their working area calm and predictable.
