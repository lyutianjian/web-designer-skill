# aesop — Aesop-Inspired Apothecary Editorial

Illustrative cues, not an official brand specification. Supplied references and accessibility needs take precedence.

**Context**: Editorial / Minimalist. Tactile consumer goods, hospitality, and product stories with a literary voice.
**Reference points**: Aesop product imagery, packaging, and store materials.

## Starting palette and type

- Ground: chamois `#E8E4D9` or cream `#F2EFE5`; surface `#F0EDE4`; ink `#1B1B1B`.
- Supporting colors: sage `#7A8470` and amber `#7A4623`, used where they reinforce the product story.
- Transitional serif options: Suisse Works, Lyon Text, or GT Sectra, with a restrained sans such as Söhne or Helvetica Now for navigation and labels.
- Body text around 16–18px with a generous line height is a useful web starting point. Keep small labels readable at the intended viewport.

## Composition cues

- Spacing example: 8 / 16 / 24 / 40 / 72 / 120; adapt the larger gaps to content and viewport.
- Square edges and quiet surfaces can keep attention on product photography. A subtle drawer transition around 450ms can fit the tone.
- Use warm surfaces, raking light, and tactile props to support the object. Give imagery and copy different but complementary amounts of space.
- A measured editorial voice and useful labels such as ingredients or ritual can establish character. Repeat labels only when they aid navigation.

## Tradeoffs

A tactile presentation needs appropriate imagery, but its typography and pacing can also inform digital products. Preserve visible pricing, clear actions, necessary overlays, and readable text rather than copying a sparse composition mechanically. Heavy contrast or playful motion may change the tone; use them when the brief benefits.
