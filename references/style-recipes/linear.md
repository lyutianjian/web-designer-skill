# linear — Linear-Inspired Product Precision

Illustrative cues, not an official brand specification. Supplied references and accessibility needs take precedence.

**Context**: Modern Tool / Builder SaaS. Focused product interfaces and developer-tool presentations.
**Reference points**: Linear product captures, marketing pages, and editorial material for the period being referenced.

## Starting palette and type

- Ground `#08090A`; surfaces `#16171C`, `#1E1F25`, and `#26272E`; fine borders such as `rgba(255,255,255,0.06)`.
- Primary text `#F7F8F8`; secondary `#9CA3AF`; muted `#6B7280`, adjusted for contrast at small sizes.
- Purple `#5E6AD2` can signal focus or a key brand moment. Its useful extent depends on the composition.
- Display options: Inter Tight, Söhne, or Geist Sans; body Inter or the project font; mono options include Geist Mono and JetBrains Mono.
- Tight display tracking and regular reading text can create precision without replacing a suitable existing type system.

## Composition cues

- Spacing example: 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 40 / 64 / 96.
- Radius examples: 6 / 12 / 16px by component role; quiet borders and restrained elevation maintain separation.
- Use aligned panels, intentional density, and clear interaction states. A real product capture can anchor a landing page.
- Shortcut chips and monospace accents work when they represent actual functionality.
- Brief ease-out feedback, with longer transitions for meaningful layout changes, can make the interface feel responsive.

## Tradeoffs

Plain Inter, gradients, imagery, larger radii, or another accent may fit the brief. Evaluate whether they preserve a coherent hierarchy rather than treating the reference as a blacklist. Adapt the tone and density when the audience needs a warmer or more introductory experience.
