# nyt-the-daily — New York Times-Inspired Editorial

Illustrative cues, not an official brand specification. Supplied references and accessibility needs take precedence.

**Context**: Information Architecture. News, long-form features, and explainers with a clear editorial hierarchy.
**Reference points**: Newspaper front pages, investigative features, and editorial typography.

## Starting palette and type

- White `#FFFFFF`; ink `#121212`; secondary text `#666666`; soft surface `#F7F7F7`; rules `#E2E2E2`.
- Red `#D0021B` can mark urgency when justified by the content.
- Headline options: a Cheltenham-like serif, Sentinel, or Tiempos Headline. Body options include Georgia, Lyon, or Source Serif.
- A restrained sans can separate interface labels from editorial text. Body text around 18–20px and line height around 1.55 are useful starting points for extended reading.

## Composition cues

- Spacing example: 4 / 8 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 96; grid relationships and fine rules can distinguish story importance.
- Kicker, headline, and standfirst work together when each carries different information.
- Include bylines, timestamps, and captions where they are meaningful and accurate.
- Pull-quotes can provide a second entry point. Vary their emphasis with the story rather than enforcing a fixed size ratio.
- Multi-column story groupings should become a clear reading order on narrow screens.

## Tradeoffs

Serif headlines and editorial grids are reference cues, not restrictions on cards, buttons, or other useful interface elements. Use alignment and line length that remain readable; justified text needs particular care on narrow screens. An authoritative appearance should be supported by the actual content and sourcing.

## Optional image study

> Editorial composition with a place-specific scene, natural lighting, neutral color grading, and room for a clear caption, 3:2. Generated imagery is illustrative; use sourced photographs for claims about real events.
