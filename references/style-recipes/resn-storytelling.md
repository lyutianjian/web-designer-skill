# resn-storytelling — Resn-Inspired Interactive Narrative

Illustrative cues, not an official brand specification. Supplied references and accessibility needs take precedence.

**Context**: Motion / Experimental. Campaign microsites, portfolios, and stories that benefit from exploration.
**Reference points**: Resn project case studies and the particular narrative interaction being referenced.

## Starting palette and type

- Derive color from the story. Warm subjects against cool environments, or the reverse, can establish contrast between narrative elements.
- Use saturation to support attention and pacing rather than keeping every scene equally intense.
- A distinctive display face can belong to the scene; supporting text needs a stable, readable treatment.
- Radius, shadow, and spacing follow the scene and product rather than a universal token set.

## Composition cues

- Give each interaction or scroll transition a narrative job: introduce, reveal, connect, or resolve.
- Scene changes can alter framing and density while retaining a clear route through the experience.
- A texture response, optional discovery, or small surprise can reward exploration without hiding essential content.
- A closing scene can resolve the story or clarify the next action. The number and duration of beats should follow the content.
- If titles are integrated into a rendered scene, preserve readable and accessible text in the page structure.

## Tradeoffs

Narrative pacing should not force users to wait for information or actions they need immediately. Provide a reduced-motion route and keep navigation understandable. Sound and hidden extras are optional; use them only when they contribute. A compact story can work with modest implementation effort if its interactions are well chosen.
