# pentagram — Pentagram / Paula Scher-Inspired Typography

Illustrative cues, not an official brand specification. Supplied references and accessibility needs take precedence.

**Context**: Information Architecture. Cultural identities, institutional pages, and messages carried by strong typography.
**Reference points**: Paula Scher's Public Theater posters and selected Pentagram identity projects; the studio's work spans many visual languages.

## Starting palette and type

- Pairing examples: black `#000000` on white `#FFFFFF`; cobalt `#1E3FFF` on cream `#F4F1E7`; brick `#B83A1F` on bone `#EFE9DC`.
- Grotesque options: Helvetica Now, Söhne Breit, or Druk for emphatic display text; use compatible reading text and tabular figures where needed.
- Large headlines can act as imagery. Set their size from copy length and available space rather than a fixed ratio or word limit.

## Composition cues

- A column grid and generous gutters, for example 24–32px, can organize bold type and quieter supporting material.
- Crisp edges, flat color fields, and restrained surfaces let the typography establish identity.
- A word, phrase, or number can become the main graphic. Cropping is useful when it remains intentional and the message is available in full.
- Align captions and metadata with the underlying grid. Rules and color blocks can mark real changes in content.
- Typographic transitions can reinforce reading order; use other imagery or motion when the brief benefits.

## Tradeoffs

Type-led composition can coexist with photography, cards, or additional type families. Dense product information may need a calmer supporting layout. A short, strong headline helps, but meaningful copy should not be discarded to fit a poster treatment.
